7 Affordable Skincare Products for a Simple Glow-Up

A good skincare routine doesn't have to be complicated or expensive. If you're just getting started, focusing on a few basic products can make your routine easier to follow and more affordable.

In this guide, we've put together seven types of skincare products worth considering for a simple everyday routine.

The Glow Edit Tip: You don't need a 10-step routine. Start with the basics and add products according to your skin's needs.

1. Gentle Cleanser

A cleanser is the first step in many skincare routines. Look for a gentle formula that cleanses without leaving your skin feeling excessively dry or tight.

What to look for:

  • Gentle cleansing ingredients
  • A formula suitable for your skin type
  • Fragrance-free options if your skin is easily irritated

Best for: Removing everyday dirt, sweat and excess oil.

2. Hydrating Moisturizer

Moisturizer helps support your skin's moisture barrier and can leave your skin feeling softer and more comfortable.

Depending on your skin's needs, look for ingredients such as glycerin, ceramides, hyaluronic acid or squalane.

Best for: Keeping skin moisturized and comfortable.

Glow Edit Tip: If you have oily skin, you may prefer a lightweight gel or lotion. If your skin is dry, a richer cream may feel more comfortable.

3. Sunscreen

Sunscreen is one of the most important daytime skincare products. For daily outdoor exposure, look for a broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher and follow the product's directions.

Best for: Helping protect skin from UV radiation.

Glow Edit Tip: A sunscreen that feels comfortable and works well under makeup can make it easier to use consistently.

4. Lip Balm

Your skincare routine doesn't have to stop at your face. A simple lip balm can help keep your lips feeling comfortable, especially in dry weather.

Look for:

  • Moisturizing ingredients
  • A texture you enjoy using
  • SPF for daytime use if desired

Best for: Everyday lip care.

5. Niacinamide Serum

Niacinamide, also known as vitamin B3, is a popular skincare ingredient found in many serums and moisturizers.

Depending on the formulation and concentration, niacinamide products may be useful for supporting the appearance of the skin barrier and improving the appearance of uneven-looking skin tone and excess oil.

Best for: People looking to add a targeted product to a basic routine.

Glow Edit Tip: If you're new to active ingredients, introduce one product at a time rather than changing your entire routine at once.

6. Body Moisturizer

Your face isn't the only part of your skin that deserves attention. A basic body moisturizer can help keep your skin feeling smooth and hydrated.

Look for:

  • Glycerin
  • Ceramides
  • Shea butter
  • Other moisturizing ingredients

Best for: Everyday body hydration.

7. Gentle Exfoliating Product

Exfoliation can be useful for some people, but more isn't necessarily better.

Depending on the product, exfoliating ingredients such as lactic acid, glycolic acid or salicylic acid can help improve the appearance and texture of the skin.

However, over-exfoliating can cause irritation.

Glow Edit Tip: Start slowly and follow the product's directions. Avoid adding several exfoliating products to your routine at the same time.

How to Build a Simple Routine

You don't need to use all seven products every day.

Morning

Cleanser → Moisturizer → Sunscreen

Evening

Cleanser → Treatment/Serum → Moisturizer

Body moisturizer and lip balm can be used whenever needed.

If you're introducing a new active skincare product, consider introducing it gradually and stop using it if it causes significant irritation.

How to Choose the Right Products

Your skin type:

Dry, oily, combination or normal skin may have different preferences.

Your main concern:

Consider whether your priority is hydration, oiliness, uneven-looking skin tone or texture.

The ingredients:

Read the product label and follow the manufacturer's directions.

Your budget:

An effective routine doesn't need to contain expensive products.

Your consistency:

A simple routine that you actually follow is generally more practical than a complicated routine you rarely use.

Final Thoughts

Building a skincare routine doesn't have to mean buying dozens of products. Start with the basics — cleanser, moisturizer and sunscreen — and add other products according to your individual needs.

The goal isn't to have the biggest skincare collection. It's to find products that fit your routine, budget and preferences.
